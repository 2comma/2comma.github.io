Beginner

You're here while you work up to a full push-up, a first chin-up or pull-up, and a full Bulgarian split squat; while daily protein is about 100 g; and while daily steps start at 5k and ramp slowly.

Protein: about 100 g a day
Steps: 5k daily for 2 weeks, then +1k every 2 weeks toward 10k
Schedule & rules
  • Schedule: 3 days a week, with a rest day between sessions. Week 1 is Mon Upper, Wed Lower, Fri Upper. Week 2 is Mon Lower, Wed Upper, Fri Lower. Keep alternating like that.
  • Session length: budget about 40 min. A 40–60 min session is for a gym routine.
  • Sets and reps: 3 sets of 6–10 reps. Once you can do 3×10 with good form, move to the next harder variation.
  • Reverse pyramid (RPT) on the first two exercises of each day: set 1 is the hardest variation you can do, set 2 is medium, set 3 is the easiest. Stop 1 rep short of failure.
  • Beginners can use holds (30 s, then 60 s, then 90 s) and negatives. Add about 5 s to a hold each time. For a negative, start at the top and lower as slowly as possible.
  • The gym scheme is 3×4–8 (4–6, then 6–8, then 8–10, move up at 3×8). This home plan uses the bodyweight 6–10 numbers. If you add dumbbells later, switch to 4–8 reps and move up at 3×8.
  • Stick to the plan for 6–8 weeks, and log every session. Leave 1–2 reps in the tank.
  • Rest: 1–4 min, longer on the hard moves. Gym sessions rest 3–5 min on the first two lifts.
  • Recommended equipment order: pull-up bar first, then a dip bar, then dumbbells.
  • Core: brace before every rep.
  • Week 1 habits, one at a time:
    1. Days 1–7, calories: work out maintenance and a deficit (see Calories). Weigh yourself every morning after the bathroom.
    2. Next 2 weeks, steps: walk 5k steps every day (see Daily movement).
    3. After that, protein: reach 100 g a day. Training still starts in week 1 at the easiest levels.
Upper day

Same five slots as the base plan. Substitutions below are only for missing equipment, plus pushing dips back in weeks 1–2.

Slot 1

Push-up variation (RPT)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10 RPT; holds, negatives, and small ranges if a full rep is hard
Equipment: none; a bench or counter for incline

Incline or knee push-ups, body straight, elbows about 45°. Holds at the start or bottom, slow negatives, very small ranges of motion.

Slot 2

Inverted rows (pull-up variation, working set)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10 RPT
Equipment: pull-up or dip bar, or a sturdy table

Start here if you have no bar yet or can't do a chin-up. Rigid plank body, pull the chest to the bar. A more diagonal body is easier than horizontal. If you have no bar, a table row works; Nerd Fitness has a public guide (How to Do a Bodyweight Row).

Slot 2

Dead hangs

Sets/reps: 3 sets for time; build toward 30–60 s, then 60–90 s
Equipment: pull-up bar

Full grip, relaxed then active shoulders. Track times.

Slot 2

Negative chin-ups

Sets/reps: 1–3 per upper day, or 3×4–5 as slow as possible
Equipment: pull-up bar and a step or chair

Jump or step to the top, lower 3–5 s under control. Also filmed on a home bar.

Slot 2

Negative chin-ups (home bar)

Sets/reps: Same as negative chin-ups
Equipment: home pull-up bar and a step

Same drill, filmed on a home bar.

Slot 2

Scapula pull-ups

Sets/reps: Controlled reps, straight arms
Equipment: pull-up bar

Straight arms, pull the shoulder blades down and back.

Slot 3

Negative dips

Sets/reps: Skip in weeks 1–2; then slow lowers only if the setup feels stable
Equipment: dip bar or 2 sturdy chairs

Start locked out, lower slowly, step up again. Feet on the floor can assist. Plan change: do not start dips in weeks 1–2.

Slot 4

Inverted rows (slot 4)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: sturdy table or bar

Second row slot. Use an easier angle than slot 2 if you already rowed, or a table row.

Slot 5

Biceps curls (superset with overhead triceps extensions)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10, supersetted
Equipment: a loaded bag or jug

Elbows fixed, no swing; start with the weaker side.

Lower day

Same five slots as the base plan, with progressions for the first full reps.

Slot 1

Bulgarian split squat (RPT; do it first)

Sets/reps: Complete beginner: lunges 3×10, then supported BSS, then mini BSS 3×10 until the first full rep. Then 1–10 per leg, back knee almost to the ground, build to 10/leg
Equipment: chair or bench; something to hold for supported BSS

Standard progression. Use the full version; supported, mini, or lunge variations are also in this slot.

Slot 2

Romanian deadlift

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: none, or a loaded bag

Soft knees, hips back, flat back, feel the hamstrings. The plan starts with the two-leg hinge, then a bag, then single-leg. The current plan lists single-leg or a bag; start with two-leg or a bag if single-leg is unstable.

Slot 2

Single-leg Romanian deadlift

Sets/reps: 3×6–10 when the two-leg hinge is stable
Equipment: none, or a bag

Hips square, hinge on the standing leg, slow. Next step after the two-leg RDL.

Slot 3

Glute bridge / hip thrust

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: none (floor), or a sofa / bench / table edge

The plan starts with a floor glute bridge, then mid-back on a bench, then single-leg. The plan table names this slot Single-leg hip thrust; start with the bridge if the single-leg version is too hard.

Slot 3

Hip thrust (bench)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: bench, sofa, or table edge

Upper back on the bench, shins vertical at the top, squeeze at the top.

Slot 4

Reverse hyperextensions (superset with Jefferson curls)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10, supersetted
Equipment: bench or table edge; no bench yet: do glute bridges here instead

Hips on the edge, lift the legs with the glutes, no low-back snap.

Slot 5

Hanging knee raises (or lying on the floor)

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: bar, or floor

No swing, curl the pelvis up, control the way down.

5 min.

At home, do 1–2 easy sets of the first exercise instead, using an easier variation. The plan also treats ATG split squats as a useful lower-body warm-up; that is optional, not a required extra.

Sets/reps: Not the home warm-up
Equipment: bar and weights (gym)

Wrist work and ramp-up sets with weights. At home, do 1–2 easy sets of the first exercise with an easier variation instead.

ATG split squat (optional lower-day warm-up)

Sets/reps: Easy, not a working set
Equipment: none, or a support to hold

Optional. The plan treats this as a useful lower-body warm-up.

Daily movement / steps

Walking is the plan's number-one cardio, and it doesn't spike hunger.

  • Walk 5k steps every day for 2 weeks. Only after that, add about 1k every 2 weeks, up to 10k.
  • Slow, easy pace. Pair walking with the 3×/week strength work so you keep muscle.
  • Do sprints or other hard cardio only on rest days. Ask your doctor first. Do not start sprints at this level unless the doctor has already cleared them.
  • Tips: a fixed walk time (morning or sunset), walk instead of short car trips, use a watch or pedometer rather than the phone alone.
Protein & eating

Target: at least 100 g of protein a day, from lean sources. Hold 100 g for a month before moving up. Later targets: 130 g (Intermediate) and 150 g (Advanced).

Lean sources: chicken breast, fish, tofu, egg whites, low-fat Greek yogurt, legumes, whey.

Saturated-fat habits to avoid or ask your doctor about:

  • a ground-beef staple meal and an "80% carnivore" phase (swap in lean poultry mince or fish)
  • 6 eggs at lunch, and the "eggs and cholesterol won't kill you" idea
  • scrambled eggs with lots of butter, and "fat and eggs for testosterone"
  • jamón, carbonara, curry roux and cheeseburgers: occasional treats only Worth keeping: a little olive oil or a spray; kimchi and seaweed; rice in a set portion; plenty of water (the plan aims for 2 L+, building toward 3–4 L — check fluid targets with your doctor); 3–5 staple meals you rotate; weighing your food for about 3 weeks to calibrate. Keep crisps and other sweet or processed snacks out of the house. Fasting: The method is to skip breakfast and eat 2 meals a day (lunch 500–1,000 kcal, dinner 1,000–2,000); training is done fasted. Weekdays use a 5–8 PM eating window; weekends use a 6–9 h window with 1.5–2 meals. Split protein across 2 servings in the weekday window. Any session before 5 PM is fasted. Ask your doctor about the window and about training fasted.

Sample 5–8 PM eating windows: about 100 g protein, low in saturated fat (estimates)

Add 150 g cooked rice, kimchi and seaweed. 7:30 PM: 200 g 0% Greek yogurt with berries (about 20 g protein). Totals: about 100 g protein, about 900 kcal.
  • B. Fish and tofu: 5 PM: salmon fillet, 150 g raw (about 30 g protein), with 200 g firm tofu (about 30 g), vegetables and potatoes. 7:30 PM: tinned tuna, 1 can (about 25 g), with beans and salad, plus a whey shake (about 20 g). Totals: about 105 g protein.
  • C. Egg-white egg rice: 5 PM: 1 whole egg plus 200 g egg whites (about 28 g protein) and 200 g chicken breast (about 62 g), with rice and soy sauce. 7:30 PM: a glass of skim milk, or a 0% yogurt (about 10 g protein). Totals: about 100 g protein. Exact calories depend on portions. Weigh the food the first few times, as a calibration step.
Calories

Setting calories:

  1. Find your maintenance with a TDEE calculator, using your weight, height, age and activity level. Most men come out at 2,500–2,700. (The Mayo Clinic calculator is one option.)
  2. Subtract 400. A range of 300–500 is fine; never go more than 500 below. That number is your daily target.
  3. Check it against your morning-weight trend over 2–3 weeks. If your weight isn't falling, lower the target, staying inside the 300–500 range.
  4. If the target comes out below 2,000 kcal, the plan says to build muscle at maintenance or a small surplus instead of cutting. Current weight, height and age are not in this package, so there is no personal calorie number here. Example only, not your numbers: maintenance 2,600 gives a target of 2,200. The calorie rule overrides food rules: surplus = gain, deficit = lose. Pair any cut with the 3×/week lifting so losses are not mostly muscle.
Sleep

Sleep basics:

  • Go to bed at a fixed time (for example, wind down at 10 PM and be in bed at 11 PM; pick your own fixed clock).
  • Keep your phone out of the bedroom.
  • Get morning sunlight.
  • No intense exercise late at night.
  • Screens off about an hour before bed. Trackers are fine if you don't obsess over them.
Optional extrasOptional

These are substitute exercises and the 4 key lower-body progressions. They are not required slots in the main routine. Use them only as substitutes or add-ons, and don't pile them onto a session until the five main movements are habitual.

ATG split squat

Sets/reps: Optional warm-up or accessory; not a main-routine slot
Equipment: none, or a support to hold

Knee far over toes, deep range, heel down. Also a lower-body warm-up.

Squat to pistol-squat progression

Sets/reps: Build toward 10 unassisted pistols; stay in a strength range
Equipment: a seat or box for the early steps

Chair/box and assisted pistols first.

Pistol squat

Sets/reps: Goal: 10 unassisted
Equipment: none

Full single-leg squat, arms forward as a counterbalance. Does not replace BSS on main-routine days.

Nordic curls

Sets/reps: Slow lowers; band assistance is fine
Equipment: something heavy to anchor the heels; optional band

Straight hips, lower as slowly as possible. Substitute exercise; not a main-routine slot.

Good mornings

Sets/reps: 3×6–10, light load
Equipment: none, or a light bag

Hip hinge, neutral spine.

Back extensions

Sets/reps: 3×6–10
Equipment: bench or table edge

Hinge at the hips, squeeze glutes at the top, don't hyperextend. Related hamstring and glute-chain demo, not the Jefferson curl.

Tibialis raises

Sets/reps: Controlled, slow
Equipment: a wall

Back to the wall, lift the toes.

Patrick step-ups

Sets/reps: Slow, controlled
Equipment: a step or low box

Heel-elevated step-down, knee over toes. Knee-health accessory.

Lateral raises

Sets/reps: Higher reps are fine (up to 20)
Equipment: light dumbbells, a loaded bag, or two books

Lead with elbows, to shoulder height, pinky level with the thumb. Not part of the main upper day; optional accessory.
