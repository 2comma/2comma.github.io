Morning Digest

  1. New York wins a $1.37 billion judgment against the founders of crypto scheme NovaTech courts A New York state court ordered NovaTech founders Cynthia and Eddy Petion to pay $1.37 billion over a cryptocurrency Ponzi scheme that targeted Haitian immigrant communities, Attorney General Letitia James said. The scheme drew in hundreds of thousands of investors worldwide, including about 11,000 New Yorkers.
  2. Standard Chartered's Zodia Custody says hackers accessed some client contact details security Zodia Custody, the crypto custodian owned by Standard Chartered, said an unauthorized party accessed limited information about some clients and employees, including business contact names and email addresses. The company said its investigation so far has found no unauthorized transactions.
  3. Bill requiring the US to hold its bitcoin for at least 20 years is cleared for a House vote policy The House Financial Services Committee formally reported a bill that would write a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into federal law, making it eligible for a vote by the full House. Bitcoin placed in the reserve could not be sold, swapped or otherwise disposed of for at least 20 years.
  4. Switzerland's first spot bitcoin ETF begins trading with 350 BTC crypto BIT Asset Management's Bitcoin Fund ETF began trading on SIX Swiss Exchange on Thursday under the ticker BBIT, holding 350 bitcoin at launch. The company says it is Switzerland's first spot bitcoin ETF and currently Europe's only one.

Spain gives its anti-money-laundering unit power to freeze crypto accounts without a judge crypto Spain's financial intelligence unit, Sepblac, will be able to freeze crypto, bank and payment accounts for up to five business days on its own initiative when it suspects money laundering or terrorist financing, under a royal decree-law published this week.
