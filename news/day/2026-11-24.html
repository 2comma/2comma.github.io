Morning Digest

  1. US adds Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian firms to its Entity List over chip diversion risk policy The Commerce Department said it is adding seven companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, including Singapore-based Megaspeed International, to the Entity List. It cited a significant risk that they could be involved in diverting advanced computing chips to unauthorized users.
  2. Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for about $41 billion in cash markets French drugmaker Sanofi said Tuesday it will pay $175 a share in cash for US biotech company Insmed, valuing it at about $41.0 billion. The price is about 46% above Insmed's closing price on Nov. 18, the day before media reports of a possible deal.
  3. HPE raises its fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to $59.4 billion to $63.1 billion after a 50% jump in quarterly sales earnings Hewlett Packard Enterprise said Monday that revenue rose 50% to $14.5 billion in its fiscal fourth quarter ended Oct. 31, with server revenue up 94.7% to $8.8 billion. It now expects fiscal 2027 revenue to grow 27% to 35%.
  4. South Korea proposes a crypto law that would let bank-led groups issue won stablecoins and cap exchange stakes at 20% regulation South Korea's Financial Services Commission (FSC) on Tuesday published a draft digital asset law under which only groups in which banks together own more than 50% could issue won-pegged stablecoins. The draft also caps any major shareholder's stake in a crypto exchange at 20%, or up to 34% with regulator approval.
  5. Anthropic launches Claude Ship, a set of AI agents that can review, fix and approve code changes ai Anthropic on Monday released Claude Ship for Claude Team and Enterprise customers with repositories on GitHub. It combines the company's existing Code Review tool with two new beta agents: PR Steward, which fixes failing checks and merge conflicts, and Claude Approvals, which can approve routine changes under a policy each team writes.
