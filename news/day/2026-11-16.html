Morning Digest

  1. US issues rule requiring licenses for remote AI-chip access above 12 million TPP regulation The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule on Monday that requires a license when advanced computing items are destined for an overseas data center that will provide more than 12 million aggregate TPP of computing power to companies headquartered in Macau or Country Group D:5. The rule also adds license requirements for advanced semiconductor shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, which it says present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion, and it formally rescinds the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule.
  2. Supreme Court rejects Custodia Bank's bid for a Fed master account regulation The Supreme Court on Monday denied Custodia Bank's petition for a writ of certiorari in Custodia Bank, Inc. v. Federal Reserve Board of Governors, No. 26-62, according to the Court's docket and its November 16 order list. The denial leaves in place a Tenth Circuit ruling that upheld the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's rejection of the Wyoming-chartered crypto bank's application for a master account.
  3. China industrial output slows to 4.8% in October; retail sales rise 0.4% economy China's value added of industrial enterprises above designated size rose 4.8% year on year in October and retail sales of consumer goods rose 0.4%, the National Bureau of Statistics said, while fixed-asset investment fell 8.1% in the first 10 months. CNBC reported that industrial output, retail sales and investment all missed economists' estimates, with industrial growth slowing from 5.5% in September.
  4. Japan's economy contracts at a 0.1% annualized rate in the third quarter economy Japan's real GDP edged down 0.0% in the July-September quarter from the previous quarter, or 0.1% at an annualized rate, according to first preliminary estimates from the Cabinet Office, the first contraction in four quarters. Private consumption fell 0.3% and net exports subtracted 0.6 percentage points, while private non-residential investment rose 1.6%.
  5. DBS and J.P. Morgan start around-the-clock US-Singapore tokenized deposit transfers markets DBS and Kinexys by J.P. Morgan said on Monday they have launched a service that lets institutional clients make instant US dollar transfers between blockchain-based accounts in the United States and Singapore, including on weekends. Ant International is the first client, the banks said in a DBS statement.
