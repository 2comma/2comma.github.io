Appeals court strikes down West Virginia law shielding judges' and officers' home addresses

The court upheld lower-court rulings in class actions a retired law enforcement officer brought against people-search services including Whitepages and LexisNexis. According to the opinion, the law is a content-based restriction on speech that is not narrowly tailored, chiefly because it imposes liability without first requiring a request to remove the information.

The ABA Journal reported that it is the first time a federal appeals court has struck down such a state law. The laws spread after the 2020 killing of Daniel Anderl, son of U.S. District Judge Esther Salas, at the family's New Jersey home.

Why it matters: Congress passed a similar protection for federal judges in 2022, and many states have their own versions, so the ruling is an early test of how courts weigh such privacy laws against free-speech rights.
