Macao court jails former lawmaker Au Kam San for 12.5 years in first national security case

The Court of First Instance said Au long helped overseas organisations collect and spread political material aimed at subverting state power, that it found most of the facts in the indictment proven, and that Au did not dispute most of them, according to a court statement published by Macau Post Daily and RTHK. The court said the subversion and foreign-links offences overlapped, so he was punished for the more severe one, subversion. It acquitted him of two other subversion counts. The court said it appointed his defence lawyer because he had not retained one.

The trial was held behind closed doors, which the court said was to protect national security interests and which AP said raised concerns about fairness. The Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office said national security is a red line that must not be crossed, RTHK reported.

Why it matters: AP reported that Au's arrest last year was the first publicly known prosecution under Macao's national security law, which took effect in 2009.
