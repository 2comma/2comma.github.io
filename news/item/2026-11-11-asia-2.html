Singapore Airlines' first-half profit falls 44% as jet fuel costs surge

Revenue rose 16.8%, driven by record passenger volumes, higher fares and strong cargo demand, while expenditure climbed more than 20% to S$10.73 billion, largely because fuel costs before hedging jumped more than 90%, CNA reported. Average jet fuel prices rose 86.7% from a year earlier amid the Middle East conflict.

The airline's share of losses from Air India narrowed to S$361.7 million from S$429.5 million, according to CNA. It declared an interim dividend of 2 Singapore cents a share and a special dividend of 5 cents.

Why it matters: The airline said jet fuel prices remain elevated compared with pre-conflict levels and continue to put significant pressure on costs, CNA reported.
