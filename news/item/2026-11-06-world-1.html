Paraguay's Congress presses impeachment of President Peña after he offers to resign over a bank collapse

Peña, in office since 2023, announced his resignation in a recorded address as protests grew over the bank's failure. Al Jazeera reported that a joint session called to consider the resignation lost its quorum after lawmakers from his Colorado Party walked out, and that the Senate opened impeachment proceedings and summoned him to appear on Friday. NPR reported that congressional leaders had initially been expected to accept the resignation.

According to Al Jazeera, Paraguay's central bank said Ueno Bank had fallen below the legal solvency ratio, and many of its roughly 3 million customers lost access to their accounts. The head of the central bank, Carlos Carvallo, has since resigned. Lawmakers have accused Peña of a conflict of interest in dealings with the bank, according to The Guardian; the allegations have not been tested in a trial.

Why it matters: AP reported that opposition senators want him removed rather than allowed to resign, because a resignation would preserve his eligibility to become a senator for life, with legal protections that could hinder prosecution. If he is removed, Vice President Pedro Alliana would likely take over, according to Al Jazeera.
