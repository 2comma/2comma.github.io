Japan's Supreme Court rules 2025 upper house vote gap was in a 'state of unconstitutionality'

Chief Justice Yukihiko Imasaki said efforts in the Diet to correct the disparity had stalled and that the gap had reached a level that could no longer be overlooked, NHK reported. The gap widened from 3.03 times in the 2022 election, according to Kyodo.

It was the court's first such finding for an upper house election since 2016, when two pairs of less-populated prefectures were merged into single constituencies to narrow the gap, Kyodo said. The court had found the 2016, 2019 and 2022 elections constitutional. Lower courts were split, with 11 of 16 rulings finding a state of unconstitutionality.

Why it matters: The last time the top court made this finding for an upper house election was for the 2013 vote, which had a gap of up to 4.77 times, Kyodo and NHK reported.
