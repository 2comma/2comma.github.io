Judge rejects Justice Department bid to have California set aside about 20,000 ballots; DOJ appeals

The Justice Department sued California earlier this month, alleging the state failed to review a list of suspected noncitizens who were sent mail-in ballots. Los Angeles officials said in court papers that a preliminary review of 200 names on the list found a "high percentage" of people who say they were born in the U.S., ABC reported.

In a late Wednesday filing, the Justice Department asked the 9th Circuit Court of Appeals to rule quickly, citing ballot processing already underway, AP reported. A 9th Circuit panel had earlier declined, 2-1, to issue an immediate order in the case.

Why it matters: The midterm elections are Tuesday, and the dispute over federal power to intervene in a state's ballot processing is now headed back to the appeals court.
