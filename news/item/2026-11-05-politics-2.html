Democrats say they will use new majorities to investigate Trump and his administration

AP reported that corruption ranked among top voter concerns in the midterm wave and that newly empowered Democrats are putting the issue at the center of their agenda. NPR and Politico said expected probes include business dealings, immigration enforcement, and other administration actions, using subpoena power once the new Congress is seated in January.

It will be the first time in Trump's current term that Democrats control both chambers, Reuters reported, raising the prospect of legislative deadlock alongside oversight fights. Exact committee priorities were still being debated among Democratic leaders, the outlets said.

Why it matters: Congressional investigative power shifts with the new majorities, setting up two years of oversight of the White House.
