Credit-card balances hit $1.16 trillion as more cardholders fall 90 days behind

U.S. bankcard balances rose 4.5% from a year earlier to $1.16 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, TransUnion said in its Credit Industry Insights Report released Nov. 5. The borrower-level share of credit-card accounts 90 or more days past due rose 13 basis points year over year to 2.50%, while the balance-level delinquency rate fell to 1.99% from 2.05%, the lowest since the third quarter of 2023. Average debt per card borrower was $6,678. Unsecured personal loan originations reached a record 8.41 million, up 22.7% year over year, with borrower-level 60-day delinquency at 4.12%. Below-prime borrowers accounted for 42.3% of recent credit-card originations and 69.0% of unsecured personal loan originations. Total outstanding consumer credit balances grew about 3% year over year, with 263 million credit-active consumers.

Why it matters: Rising borrower-level card delinquency means more households are missing payments even as lenders keep overdue balances smaller. Record personal-loan originations and a larger below-prime share point to wider credit access that can raise monthly debt payments for higher-risk borrowers.
