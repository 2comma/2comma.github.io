Labor Department publishes rule requiring drug-benefit managers to disclose fees to employer plans

The rule, which the department finalized on October 29, requires pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) to disclose manufacturer rebates and other payments, compensation earned when a plan pays more for a drug than the pharmacy is reimbursed, and payments recouped from pharmacies, AHA News reported. It also lets plan fiduciaries audit the disclosures. The rule takes effect January 4, 2027, and applies to plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027.

Why it matters: The department says pharmacy spending is a major driver of employers' health costs and is growing faster than medical and surgical spending. The disclosures are meant to help plans judge whether what they pay PBMs is reasonable.
