Historic liner SS United States is sunk off Florida to become an artificial reef

After hours of slow descent, the nearly 1,000-foot ship disappeared beneath the surface at 10 p.m. ET and now rests upright about 180 feet down, NPR reported. Okaloosa County, Florida, bought the ship in 2024 and decided to sink it, and the work was led by marine contractor Coleen Marine, according to NPR.

The liner took its maiden voyage in 1952 and set a trans-Atlantic speed record that still stands, NPR said. The SS United States Conservancy, which had hoped to turn the ship into a hotel or museum, plans to build a museum in its honor in Florida.

Why it matters: The ship's rooms and corridors are set to become a dive site and habitat for marine life, NPR reported.
