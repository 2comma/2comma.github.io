Friday · Nov 6, 2026 · Asia Digest · policy

Japan's lower house passes bill to cut the consumption tax on food to 1% for two years

The bill passed with votes from the Liberal Democratic Party and the Japan Innovation Party, while all opposition parties voted against it, NHK reported. Former Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba and three other LDP lawmakers left the chamber before the vote, the Asahi Shimbun reported. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told a committee that at least 30% of workers would be eligible for the payments, the Japan Times and Jiji Press reported. The government has said it would not rely on deficit-financing bonds to fund the measures, NHK and the Japan Times reported.

The ruling parties aim to enact the bill before the Diet session ends on Dec. 12, Kyodo and Jiji Press reported, and hope to start upper house deliberations as early as Wednesday, Jiji Press said.

Why it matters: Jiji Press described the cut as a temporary measure to address soaring prices. The ruling coalition has a majority in the lower house but not in the upper house, NHK and the Japan Times reported.
