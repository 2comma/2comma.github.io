Supreme Court lets border wall construction resume in Texas' Big Bend region

In an unsigned order, the court said the government is very likely to win its argument that the district court lacked jurisdiction, because the challengers' major-questions claim alleged that the Homeland Security secretary exceeded his statutory authority rather than a constitutional violation, the only type of claim the 1996 law allows. The justices declined the government's request to summarily reverse the lower court.

The challengers, a river guide, an environmental nonprofit and a group preserving a historic church in Ruidosa, Texas, contested waivers of environmental and conservation laws issued for the project, according to the order. NBC News reported that the government says the section would involve 341 miles of construction.

Why it matters: The court wrote that the government's border-security interest "outweighs any aesthetic, recreational, and environmental harm" alleged by the challengers, AFP reported, clearing the way for work to restart while the case continues.
