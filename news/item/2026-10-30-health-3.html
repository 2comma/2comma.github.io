WHO certifies Barbados for eliminating mother-to-child transmission of HIV

An independent assessment found that 98% of pregnant women received antenatal care, 98% were tested for HIV and 98% of pregnant women living with HIV received antiretroviral treatment over the three-year period, the Pan American Health Organization said. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said Barbados provides these services free to every pregnant woman, regardless of nationality.

Barbados is now one of 26 countries and territories recognized by WHO for eliminating, or being on track to eliminate, mother-to-child transmission of HIV, syphilis or hepatitis B. Fifteen of them are in the Americas. UNAIDS also welcomed the certification.

Why it matters: The certification means an independent review confirmed that Barbados met WHO's global targets for keeping babies from being born with HIV.
