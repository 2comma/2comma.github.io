Fed officials signal more rate hikes after a hotter-than-expected October inflation report

In a speech in London on Tuesday before the data were released, Waller said he would support at least a 25 basis point increase in December and that hiking only that much after another hot reading "would put our credibility at risk." Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said she sees a need for two more quarter-point increases, CNBC reported.

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the report "borderline disturbing" in a PBS NewsHour interview, while Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said "further gradual tightening seems appropriate," according to CNBC. The Fed raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point in September to a range of 3.75% to 4% and held it in October.

Why it matters: Waller, Logan and Paulson vote on the Fed's rate-setting committee this year, and its final meeting of the year is in December, CNBC noted.
